How I actually work

I'm an INFJ, which shows up in how I approach almost everything I study. I need to sit with a problem alone first — reading around it, taking messy notes, turning it over a few times before it makes sense to me. Only after that do I bring it to my coursemates, partly to check my thinking and partly because talking it through out loud catches gaps that quiet reading doesn't. My days are usually split fairly evenly between long solo study blocks and time spent with classmates, either grinding through assignments together or just decompressing after a heavy one.

SPM: 9A in 10 subjects

Before MMU, there was SPM — and I came out of it with 9 A's out of 10 subjects. It's still one of the results I'm quietly proudest of, not because it was flashy, but because of how unglamorous the work behind it actually was. Long stretches of revision on days I didn't feel like opening a book, past papers redone until the mistakes stopped repeating, that kind of thing. The one subject that didn't turn into an A still nags at me a little if I'm honest, but I've stopped reading it as a flaw in the result and started reading it as a reminder that consistency beats any single good week. That result is a big part of why I trust the "just put the reps in" approach I'm carrying into university coursework now.

Why cybersecurity, specifically

I like the idea of being the person who notices something is off before it turns into an actual problem. That's what pulled me toward a SOC analyst role in particular — it's less about breaking things and more about attention, patience, and pattern recognition, which happen to be things I already gravitate toward. I see it as the right place to start a security career: get genuinely comfortable reading systems, logs, and traffic first, and only move into penetration testing once I actually understand what I'd be trying to break and why. Skipping straight to the exciting part felt like it would leave gaps I'd regret later.
